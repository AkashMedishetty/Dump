# STANDBY — PurpleHat Events brand film (dark-keynote grammar)

40 s · 1920×1080 and 1080×1920 · 24 fps · no voice (on-screen type carries it) · −14 LUFS
Use: LinkedIn / Instagram brand post, pitch-deck opener, website hero loop, pre-show screen at our own events.

## Three structures considered
1. **Countdown** — T-90 days → doors open, one checklist item per beat. Clear, but it is every agency's "timeline" video.
2. **Zoom ladder** — one cue dot → a cue row → a run-of-show → a venue → a city of events. Strong scale, weak turn.
3. **Noise → one call → order** — the invisible mess behind one event is *generated* until it is unbearable, time freezes, one violet cue goes from STANDBY to GO, and everything snaps into one run of show. **Chosen**: it has a real turn, it is what a client actually buys from us (one accountable call), and the medium does the turn natively (freeze + snap grid).

## Logline and arc
Two thousand four hundred messages stand behind one three-day conference; it takes one call to make all of them move.
Setup: the number, then the noise it stands for. Turn: freeze → STANDBY → GO. Ending: everything is put away until only the violet cue is left, and it becomes the lit quarter of the PurpleHat mark.

## Benchmark
- Learn from launch keynotes of software companies (the lone giant number, one line per screen, light as the reveal) and from stage-management practice itself: the cue light, "standby / go", the calm voice of a show caller.
- Learn from Steve Reich-style process music: the score is a visible process (accelerate by dividing the beat, then everything lands on one chord, then subtraction).
- Don't take: any company's UI, fonts, sounds, logos, shot sequences. All UI is drawn in code and original.

## Carry-over principle
One element crosses every cut: the violet cue dot. It is born in the freeze, it is the GO, it is the playhead on the run of show, it is the last thing left, it becomes the logo's lit arc. Every transition is a UI element expanding or the frame collapsing into one. No fades, no blank frames.

## Cue map
- 120 bpm, 4/4. Bars 1–2 number (sine pulse + digit grains on 16ths). Bars 3–6 noise: scattered detuned plucks, random pans, beat divided 4 → 8 → 16 → 32 per bar; coil whine rises. Bar 7 beat 1: freeze → digital zero for 2 beats. Bar 7 beat 3: dot born (felt mallet + sine drop). Bars 7–8 standby: one marimba note per beat, dry. Bar 9 beat 1: GO (low hit + shimmer chord, D lydian); landings are marimba notes from one chord, centred. Bars 11–14 track: phasing marimba pair + soft pad + shaker; each cue GO a vibraphone note. Bars 14–16 number back to 1: sine pulse returns. Bars 16–18 put-away: subtractive (a note removed every beat). Bars 18–20 end card: one bell on the dot's last blink, pad tail, then a room tone and silence.
